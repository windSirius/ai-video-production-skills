# Narrative editing quality rubric

## Scoring

Score each category from 0 to its maximum. Cite exact timestamps and observable evidence. Do not award points for intent that is not visible or audible.

| Category | Maximum | Test |
|---|---:|---|
| Narrative contract and hook | 15 | Does the opening match the title/cover promise, establish stakes, and create a specific question without confusing orientation? |
| Visual reasoning | 20 | Does each important visual advance, explain, prove, contrast, reveal, or emotionally reframe the narration? |
| Rhythm and attention | 15 | Do density and shot duration change with narrative function, with cuts motivated by meaning, action, gaze, or sound? |
| Emotional architecture | 15 | Does the edit build, turn, release, and resolve emotion rather than maintaining one intensity? |
| Evidence and comprehension | 10 | Are claims attributable, exact proof highlighted, and cards held long enough to understand? |
| Sound and narration | 10 | Is narration dominant and intelligible; do music, silence, ambience, and effects support chapters and emphasis? |
| Continuity and payoff | 10 | Are spatial, character, motif, and argumentative relationships coherent, with meaningful callbacks or resolution? |
| Technical finish | 5 | Are identity, captions, source fidelity, frame edges, timing, resolution, cover assets, auxiliary tracks and tail clean? |

Total: 100.

Interpretation:

- **90–100**: exceptional internal benchmark; still requires external evidence before a “top-tier” claim.
- **80–89**: strong and publishable, with localized improvement opportunities.
- **70–79**: competent but contains structural or experiential weaknesses.
- **Below 70**: revise the concept or sequence before polishing.

## Hard gates

Any failed hard gate blocks acceptance regardless of score:

- wrong character, event, source, quotation, or factual attribution;
- narration masked or materially unintelligible;
- essential evidence unreadable at delivery size;
- opening makes a promise the video does not fulfill;
- missing media, unintended black frame/tail, broken transition, or visible draft failure;
- unlicensed or unauthorized material used outside the user's permitted workflow;
- irreversible overwrite of the only recoverable draft or picture master.
- an official character face, silhouette, costume, model or named identity is malformed, invented, or inconsistent with the frozen source;
- B/C assets were rendered without review, or audience-facing cards expose internal source IDs/review warnings;
- mixed UID-visible/full-frame and UID-hidden/cropped gameplay in a house-style master;
- a renderer/cache/concat artifact creates even a single unintended empty black, green or transparent frame at a cut;
- cover text or named subjects fail at platform thumbnail size.

## Diagnostic questions

For every weak interval, ask in this order:

1. Is the narration itself doing clear narrative work?
2. What should the viewer understand or feel by the end of the interval?
3. What function does the current visual perform?
4. Is the problem shot choice, order, duration, sound, typography, or missing evidence?
5. What is the smallest intervention that tests the diagnosis?

Do not prescribe faster cuts until the function and comprehension questions are answered.

## External validation

Internal scores are repeatable revision aids, not audience truth. When available, record:

- first-30-second retention relative to similar-length channel videos;
- timestamped dips, spikes, and top moments;
- average view duration and completion behavior;
- client/user acceptance changes;
- exact reviewer rejection categories and whether the smallest verified patch resolved them;
- whether a learned rule improved more than one project.

Do not use a single video's analytics to create a universal threshold.
