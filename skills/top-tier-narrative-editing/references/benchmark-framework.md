# Benchmark framework

## Purpose

Build a diverse evidence base for excellent narrative editing without collapsing quality into popularity or copying one creator's surface style.

## Selection filters

Admit a work to the active benchmark only when it satisfies most of these:

- close relevance to game narrative, character study, documentary explanation, or audiovisual essay;
- visible command of structure, visual reasoning, sound, evidence, or emotional pacing;
- independent recognition, repeated high-quality output, or a clearly documented craft process;
- enough accessible material to analyze the claimed trait;
- no dependence on a single spectacular source clip for its effect;
- a mechanism that can plausibly transfer to the target production constraints.

Record negative references too. A work can be popular and still be useful mainly as a failure comparison.

## Corpus strata

Maintain multiple strata so one mode does not dominate:

1. **Game and character essays** — close match for Alan's narration-led subject matter.
2. **Compact visual arguments** — learn how image and sound perform reasoning instead of merely decorating narration.
3. **Evidence-led documentaries** — learn attribution, maps, archives, screenshots, and source readability.
4. **Emotional long-form essays** — learn motif, vulnerability, escalation, and reflective pacing.
5. **High-retention openings** — study expectation matching and narrative contracts, not indiscriminate speed.
6. **Sound-led sequences** — study bridges, silence, chapter changes, and audiovisual emphasis.

## User-designated core corpus

When the user names specific creators, seasons, playlists, or collections as the learning target, register them as `core-corpus`. Give them priority for domain vocabulary, audience expectations, platform conventions, and representative sampling.

Do not assume every item in a core corpus is top-tier. Rank and sample within the collection, identify both strong and weak cases, and require individual audiovisual evidence before promoting a mechanism. Use independently recognized works as calibration references rather than replacements for the user's chosen domain.

## Seed evidence

These are starting points, not a closed canon:

- **Jacob Geller** is a high-relevance game/video-essay reference. His official site documents repeated inclusion in Sight and Sound's annual best-video-essay lists and describes his focus on games, art, politics, and storytelling: https://jacobgeller.com/
- **“Fantasies of Nuremberg”** is a useful current case for research, primary-source integration, vulnerability, and visceral editorial choices. Sight and Sound's 2025 poll explicitly identifies those strengths: https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/best-video-essays-2025
- **Every Frame a Painting** is a reference for compact audiovisual argument and editing-as-explanation. The creators' EditFest session specifically concerns shaping compelling essays for online audiences: https://editfestglobal.com/2025-editfest-editing-essays-for-the-internet/
- **Sight and Sound's annual poll** is a discovery pool, not an automatic quality label. Its 2025 edition includes 255 works nominated by 72 international voters across academic, festival, gallery, social, and YouTube ecosystems: https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/polls/best-video-essays-2025
- **YouTube retention analytics** provides outcome evidence. Use intro retention, top moments, spikes, and dips relative to similar-length channel baselines: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415

Add Chinese-language and platform-specific references before claiming the corpus represents the target audience. Do not assume English-language critical acclaim transfers unchanged to Bilibili or Chinese gaming audiences.

## Reference annotation

For each work, record:

- creator, title, URL or legal local path, publication date, duration, and language;
- why it belongs in the corpus and which stratum it represents;
- narrative-unit timestamps;
- opening contract and time to central question;
- visual function at each sampled unit;
- proof/claim relationship and evidence-reading duration;
- shot-density changes by narrative function;
- music, silence, ambience, and sound bridges;
- recurring motifs and payoff;
- one transferable hypothesis;
- one surface trait that must not be copied;
- confidence and competing explanation.

## Sampling rule

Do not fully annotate every minute of every work at first. Analyze:

- first frame and first 30 seconds;
- first explicit claim;
- first proof sequence;
- one transition between chapters;
- emotional turn;
- climax;
- final 30 seconds.

Expand to full annotation only when the sampled work contains a mechanism worth testing.
